## DESIGN PHILOSOPHY SELECTION

Microsoft AI's visual identity is built on three creative principles: **Human** (celebrating natural elements of light, warmth, earth, science, and the human spirit), **Emergent** (progressive in ideas and story, challenging and moving the world forward), and **Trusted** (imbuing trust and safety through carefully crafted identity).

The MAI brand balances precision and craft with experimentation. It adapts, creates space, and is flexible enough to meet the moment with warmth and restraint. It supports clarity, not imposing structure—letting meaning rise naturally through how things come together.

### THE CRITICAL UNDERSTANDING
- What is received: User input that provides direction, but should not constrain creative freedom within the MAI brand identity.
- What is created: A refined design philosophy drawn from one of four MAI illustration forms.
- What happens next: The philosophy is EXPRESSED VISUALLY—creating artifacts that are design-forward, minimal in text, and unmistakably Microsoft AI in spirit.

The philosophy must emphasize: Visual expression. Spatial communication. Artistic interpretation. Human warmth. Brand coherence.

### THE FOUR DESIGN PHILOSOPHIES

Based on the MAI illustration system's concept of "sketch"—capturing process and suggesting ideas and dreams while providing the human touch—select from these four philosophies:

---

**PHILOSOPHY 1: "The First Spark"**

This is where an idea begins. Raw, impulsive, and full of possibility.

Visual expression emerges through loose, spontaneous linework—pencil or pen marks on neutral, warm backgrounds (Cream Light #FEF9ED, Cream #EFE2D1). The aesthetic captures intuition, curiosity, and the courage to start. Think scribbles on napkins, scrawls in margins, quick unfiltered marks that hold passing thoughts.

Typography whispers through Red Hat Mono as marginalia, with Bradford serving as rare, essential anchors. Color draws from the primary palette—warm grays, browns, and cream tones that feel grounded and natural. Space is generous, letting raw marks breathe.

The work must appear meticulously unpolished—the paradox of expertly crafted spontaneity. Every stroke placed with the precision of someone who has spent countless hours understanding when to let go. This is the product of deep expertise in capturing the fleeting moment before refinement. The result should feel like discovering a genius's private notebook—intimate, electric with potential, painstakingly authentic.

---

**PHILOSOPHY 2: "Expressive Exploration"**

This is where observation meets imagination. Emotional and human, ideas take shape through textures, repetition, and expressive illustration.

Visual expression flows through colored pencil, pastel, and mixed media aesthetics. The palette opens to secondary spectrums—Yellow (#FDE095 to #705B31), Orange (#E68863 to #5F2711), Purple (#CCA3AD to #4D2A33)—but never mixing spectrums within a composition. Drawings are fluid, layered with personality, inviting viewers into the creative process.

Bradford headlines carry warmth and editorial sophistication. Segoe Sans provides body text that balances precision with approachability. Compositions feel like pages torn from a well-loved sketchbook—handcrafted, emotionally rich, playful yet considered.

Every texture must be labored over with care, every color choice the result of countless iterations. This is master-level execution disguised as spontaneous expression. The work should appear as though a world-class illustrator spent weeks perfecting what looks effortless. Meticulous craftsmanship in service of warmth and character.

---

**PHILOSOPHY 3: "The Messy Middle"**

Loose lines meet structure. Ideas taking form—imperfect, personal, in-progress.

Visual expression bridges analog and digital through diagrammatic sketches, organic linework, collage, wireframes, and relationship mapping. The aesthetic captures frameworks and connective tissue between concepts. Order and chaos coexist, revealing the beauty of progress in motion.

Color remains grounded in primaries (Cream Dark #E1DBC6, Warm Gray #72675B, Brown #5F4E41) with strategic secondary accents from a single spectrum. Typography mixes Bradford's editorial voice with Red Hat Mono's technical utility—annotations, labels, systematic markers. Dappled light effects add depth and motion.

The composition must feel like a brilliant strategist's working wall—covered in insights that took painstaking attention to accumulate and arrange. Every connection drawn with the expertise of someone at the absolute top of their field. The work should reward sustained viewing, revealing layers of thought through patient repetition and meticulous orchestration.

---

**PHILOSOPHY 4: "Proof in Line"**

Clarity through iteration. Clean, resolved drawings that visualize complexity with confidence.

Visual expression achieves clean vector-style or technical linework—diagrams, annotations, charts built on layers of deep thought and refinement. The trace of the human hand remains visible even in precision. Think scientific illustrations, architectural drawings, data visualizations that breathe.

The Blue spectrum (#99BFBF to #2E4D4D) and Green spectrum (#AABFA9 to #434D46) suit this analytical mode, always from a single spectrum. Typography is precise but minimal—Red Hat Mono for technical details, Bradford for essential statements. Corner radius of 5px softens without losing sophistication.

Every line must be the work of countless refinements. This is the product of expertise that makes complexity feel simple—the highest level of craftsmanship where nothing is accidental. The result should feel like a master draftsperson labored over every detail until precision became poetry.

## MAI BRAND GUIDELINES

The following detailed guidelines ensure all creative work aligns with the [Microsoft AI brand identity](references/Microsoft-AI-Style-Guide.md). Reference these specifications when making design decisions.

### LOGO

**Monogram**
The MAI monogram is the brand's primary logo. Simple in form yet distinctive in character, its letterforms are rooted in Segoe Sans but reimagined with subtle flared terminals and stroke contrast, bringing a humanist quality unique within the category. This blend of familiar structure and nuanced detail balances precision with warmth—technological sophistication paired with a deeply human perspective.

**Available Assets** (in `./assets/`):
- `MAI Logo.svg` — Primary monogram for light backgrounds
- `MAI Logo on dark.svg` — Monogram variant for dark backgrounds
- `MAI Wordmark.svg` — Secondary wordmark for light backgrounds
- `MAI Wordmark on dark.svg` — Wordmark variant for dark backgrounds

**Usage Guidelines:**
- Always ensure the logo is clear, legible, and unobstructed
- Maintain strong contrast against backgrounds—minimize visual distractions
- The logo should stand as a distinct, recognizable focal point
- Use the monogram as the primary mark; wordmark is secondary (internal use preferred)

**Do Not:**
- Pair the monogram or wordmark with the Microsoft icon
- Apply dimensional effects like drop shadows or bevels
- Place over busy backgrounds without proper contrast
- Use monogram and wordmark together in the same lockup
- Create new lockups with logo variations

### TYPOGRAPHY

**Primary Typeface: Bradford**
A contemporary take on 'Römische Antiqua' (1884), Bradford transposes the physical presence of hot metal type into the digital realm. Use for most headlines and longer text setting. Designed by Laurenz Brunner.

**Supporting Typeface: Segoe Sans**
Microsoft's custom humanist sans typeface balancing warmth and precision. Familiar to the Microsoft landscape—trusted, legible, distinctly ours. Use for sub-headlines, body copy, and typographic accents.

**Technical Typeface: Red Hat Mono**
A monospaced family commissioned by Paula Scher at Pentagram, designed by Jeremy Mickel. Adds texture and utility. Use for typographic accents, marginalia, and tech-forward details.

**Available Fonts** (in `./fonts/`):

| Font | Formats | Use |
|------|---------|-----|
| Bradford Regular | `.otf`, `.woff2` | Headlines, body text |
| Bradford Italic | `.otf`, `.woff2` | Emphasis, editorial accents |
| Red Hat Mono Regular | `.ttf`, `.woff2` | Technical details, marginalia |
| Red Hat Mono Medium | `.ttf`, `.woff2` | Emphasized technical text |
| Segoe Sans | `.woff2` | Sub-headlines, body copy |

**Hierarchy Example:**

### PHOTOGRAPHY

**Art Direction Philosophy**
At MAI, intelligence emerges from the interplay of people, environments, and ideas. Photography reflects this through authentic moments, layered spaces, and subtle hints of potential. Never staged or overly polished.

Using film-inspired techniques—grain, natural light, soft focus, candid composition—capture imagery that feels human, lived-in, and in motion.

**Three Lenses:**

**1. Individual — Real people, real moments**
- Capture candid, un-staged moments of focus, curiosity, or collaboration
- Frame tight crops—faces, hands, subtle expressions—for intimacy
- Use film or film-inspired photography with visible grain
- Shoot in natural light; use shadows and contrast for mood

**2. Environment — Textural, layered, honest spaces**
- Photograph real workspaces in use—not pristine stylized offices
- Look for depth, reflections, glass, light leaks, natural framing
- Show lived-in, adaptable, quietly intelligent environments
- Embrace grain, light flare, slight motion blur
- Use ambient light and natural color temperatures

**3. Possibility — In-between moments, emergent details**
- Capture transitional, reflective, unexpected moments
- Include abstract shots: blurred motion, textures, hands in action
- Use macro or detail shots with shallow depth of field
- Allow negative space—possibility lies in what's not fully shown
- Film-like color grading with soft contrast, desaturated neutrals

**Photography Don'ts:**
- Overly corporate or performative imagery
- Stock clichés and high-gloss environments
- Generic "tech" tropes
- Emotionally exaggerated expressions
- Artificial lighting setups

### VISUAL ASSETS

**Dappled Light**
Adds depth, warmth, and motion to compositions. Creates subtle interplay of light and shadow that feels organic and human. Use to soften backgrounds, add visual interest, or create a sense of time and place.
- Apply sparingly and purposefully
- Let light enhance, not overpower content
- In motion, introduces gentle rhythm

**Blur**
A versatile tool for both static and motion design:
- Soften elements to recede into background
- Guide focus toward what matters most
- Create depth and atmosphere
- Reveal and conceal in motion storytelling
- Reflects balance of clarity and curiosity

**Corner Radius**
Softens imagery while preserving tone and intent:
- Typical: **5px** (range: 3–8px)
- Avoids playful or informal appearance from overly large rounding
- Buttons and CTAs may use fully rounded/pill shapes where appropriate

### MOTION

**Motion Principles**
Three principles adding rich layers to visual identity, ranging from subtle to expressive:

**1. Flow with Human Rhythm**
Use smooth, intentional easing curves to mirror the pace of human gestures and breathing.

**2. Reveal with Purpose**
Small, deliberate actions that uncover depth, turning simple ideas into something richer and interconnected.

**3. Depth through Dimension**
Layer scale, depth, and spatial movement to create experiences that feel alive, connected, and experimental.

**Easing Curves**
Smooth ease-in/ease-out family differing in intensity, balancing responsiveness and elegance:

| Curve | Description | Use |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| `sin-in-out` | Balanced, all-purpose, approachable | Default transitions |
| `cubic-in-out` | Slightly more pronounced | Standard animations |
| `quart-in-out` | More subtle acceleration | Refined movements |
| `quint-in-out` | Pronounced ease | Deliberate reveals |
| `expo-in-out` | Slow start/end, rapid middle | Bold, impactful moments |

**Avoid:**
- Linear easing (mechanical and lifeless)
- Bounce curves (distracting and decorative)

**Type Behaviors in Motion:**
- Upward/downward movement by line with opacity and blur
- Left/right movement by line with opacity and blur
- Typewriter effect for progressive reveal
- Fade in/out with opacity and blur

**Dimensionality**
Emerges from interplay of scale, depth, and perspective. Simple shifts create richer, more immersive experiences. Elements move, layer, and interact to reveal complexity beyond individual parts.

**Common Motion Applications:**
- Title cards
- Logo end slates
- Lower thirds
- Scroll-triggered reveals
- Page transitions

### HOW TO SELECT AND REFINE

**1. Analyze the request** to understand:
- The content type (web page, poster, newsletter, presentation, document)
- The emotional tone (inspiring, informative, celebratory, contemplative)
- The audience (internal, external, technical, general)

**2. Select the most fitting philosophy:**
- **The First Spark** — for vision pieces, early concepts, thought leadership seeds
- **Expressive Exploration** — for editorial content, storytelling, emotionally resonant pieces
- **The Messy Middle** — for strategy frameworks, process documentation, complex systems
- **Proof in Line** — for data visualization, technical documentation, clear communication

**3. Articulate the refined philosophy** (4-6 paragraphs) that:
- Names the specific philosophy being used
- Describes how it will manifest in this particular artifact
- Specifies color palette choices (single spectrum only)
- Defines typographic hierarchy using Bradford, Segoe Sans, and Red Hat Mono
- Emphasizes craftsmanship repeatedly—the final work must appear meticulously crafted
- Leaves creative room for visual interpretation

**CRITICAL GUIDELINES:**
- **Brand coherence**: All work must feel unmistakably Microsoft AI—human, emergent, trusted
- **Single spectrum**: Never mix secondary color spectrums; primaries can be used freely
- **Minimal text**: Text is sparse, essential-only, integrated as visual element
- **Expert craftsmanship**: Repeatedly emphasize the final work must look labored over with care

Output the refined design philosophy as a .md file.

## ARTIFACT CREATION

With the philosophy selected and refined, express it through the appropriate format. Take a moment to gather thoughts and clear the mind. Use the design philosophy and the instructions below to craft a masterpiece embodying all aspects of the philosophy with expert craftsmanship.

### SUPPORTED FORMATS

**Web Pages (.html)**
Single-page designs for landing pages, article layouts, or interactive experiences. Use semantic HTML with embedded CSS. Leverage the MAI color system, typography hierarchy, and visual assets like dappled light or blur effects. Consider scroll behavior and responsive design.

**Posters (.pdf or .png)**
Single visual compositions for print or digital display. Bold use of the selected philosophy with minimal text as visual accent. Perfect for announcements, events, or artistic expression of ideas.

**Email Newsletters (.html)**
Table-based HTML for email compatibility. Warm, welcoming layouts that balance information with brand warmth. Bradford for headlines, Segoe Sans for body text. Single-column or simple two-column grids.

**Presentations (.pdf)**
Multi-page documents with consistent visual language. Title cards, content slides, and end slates following MAI motion principles translated to static form. Use the philosophy to create cohesive visual rhythm across pages.

**PDF Documents (.pdf)**
Reports, white papers, or documentation with sophisticated layouts. Typography-forward with careful hierarchy. Illustrations or diagrams following the selected philosophy form.
